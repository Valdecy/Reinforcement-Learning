Training

ε falls from 1 to 0 over the first 60% of the steps. Each training episode is one ball on a fresh wall. The state is where the ball is relative to the paddle, whether it is falling, how high it is, its sideways speed and its distance from the side walls, cut into bins. The bricks are not part of it.

On the machine's court, dashed lines mark the height and side bins it sees. The strip under its paddle slices its Q-table for the ball as it is now: each cell is a place the paddle's centre could be, split into Q(left), Q(stay) and Q(right), tinted green for positive and red for negative. The chalk mark is the move it makes there. Point at a cell to read its values.

ε = 0.00

Chance, on every tick, that the machine ignores its Q-table and moves at random. Even at 0 it drops the odd ball, and it never aims at the bricks that are left.

Game speed

Each of you has 3 balls, the same wall and the same serves. Clear your wall first and you win; otherwise most bricks wins, and the match stops as soon as the result can no longer change. Level when both are out, or when the 3-minute clock runs down, is a draw.

← → or A D move your paddle, as do the buttons under the board, the mouse over your court or a drag. Space serves; P pauses.
