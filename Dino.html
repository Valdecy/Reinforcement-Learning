Training

ε falls from 1 to 0 over the first 60% of the steps. Every training run gets a fresh random desert, and each jump costs a little, so the dino learns to jump only when it has to. The state is the next obstacle's kind, how many ticks until the dino reaches it, the dino's height, whether it is rising, and the running speed, cut into bins.

The strip under the machine's lane slices its Q-table for its next obstacle, its height and its speed: each cell is how far ahead that obstacle could be, split into Q(run), Q(jump) and Q(duck), tinted green for positive and red for negative. The chalk mark is the move it makes there: a dot to run, a chevron up to jump, down to duck. Point at a cell to read its values.

ε = 0.00

Chance, on every tick, that the machine ignores its Q-table and runs, jumps or ducks at random. At 0, a well-trained dino seldom crashes, so the best you can usually do is a draw.

Game speed

Both dinosaurs run the same desert at the same speed, which creeps up as they go. The first to hit a cactus or a pterodactyl loses. Crossing the finish line together, or crashing on the same tick, is a draw.

Space, ↑ or W jumps (hold to keep hopping); ↓ or S ducks, or dives in mid-air. A tap on the board jumps. P pauses.
